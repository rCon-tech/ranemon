# Ranemon: The London Expansion (story)

Story only, written 24 October 2026. Nothing here is built yet: no maps, code or art. It picks up after I Love Techno in Ghent and runs as a second adventure, the way the handhelds' later regions do.

Working title: **Ranemon: Free the Lads**.

Every new place in it (Dublin Airport, Stansted, London, the Premier League towns and Buckingham Palace) is **2D only**, drawn flat even with the 3D look picked, like Ghent and the I Love Techno hall. Brighton already exists and stays as it is.

## Cast

### DJ Próvaí

The DJ of Kneecap, and your guide for the whole expansion. Drawn from Rich's reference photo:

- a hand-knitted **tricolour balaclava**: green on his left, white down the middle, orange on his right, chunky rib knit, only his eyes and mouth showing;
- a **black and very dark green boiler-suit style tracksuit**, zipped to the neck, with a belt at the waist;
- **red piping** down the sleeves and sides;
- **white Celtic-knot panels** running along the shoulders and down the sleeves and legs;
- a small **tricolour pin** on the chest, and a microphone in his hand.

He talks fast, half in Irish, never takes the balaclava off (not even to drink: he has a straw) and treats the whole thing as a gig that's running late. He fights alongside nobody and hands out no free wins: he's the one who explains, cheers and occasionally heckles.

### Mo Chara and Móglaí Bap

The other two members of Kneecap. Off-screen hostages for most of the story, seen in the cutscene where they're taken (Chapter 5) and freed in the Throne Room (Chapter 11). Mo Chara is the one who keeps trying to start a song in the cage; Móglaí Bap keeps telling him to wait for the DJ.

### Returning

- **You**, the Champion of Ireland, back from Ghent with a party you're about to lose at the border.
- **Ollie** (very French) and **Ollie's parents**, Monsieur and Madame, who pay for your Ranemon to be minded while you're away and send updates after every jersey.
- **Michael D Higgins**, whose farm in Clare minds the Ranemon. He phones once, near the end.
- **Margaret Thatcher**, the rival, delighted to finally be home. After you beat her on the quays in Dublin she went home for a proper cup of tea, lost her Ranemon to Border Force at Stansted like everyone else, and now wants an audience with the King, which she has been queueing for since 1979. She picks after you, with a type edge, as before.
- **Carl Ryan and Elvis**, Stead, Kill, Enda, Cramps, Paul Mescal and Alex Simonin, in cameos (see Side quests and the ending).

### New in London

- **Sgt. Pemberton, UK Border Force**, who explains the rules nobody has read.
- **Florence Pugh**, behind the bar in every W.B Yates, as Dua Lipa is in every McGrattans. She heals your party (see **The W.B Yates pubs**). Liam Neeson had the job first; he's now a side quest up Big Ben.
- **Donal**, who runs W.B Yates London: a Corkman who's been here since 1987 and still says he's going home next year. Each W.B Yates is run by a different Irish family living in England.
- **The 12 club legends** (see the Premier League table).
- **The Royal Family**: Prince Edward, Princess Anne, Prince William, Prince Andrew, Zombie Queen Elizabeth, King Charles, and Queen Camilla at his side (she doesn't fight). Prince Harry has left a note.
- **Bridie from Ballina**, a palace cleaner, and the rest of the palace's Irish staff.

## Chapter outline

### Chapter 1: Trouble in London (Connolly Station, Dublin)

Starts the first time you step off the Eurostar from Ghent with `techno:done` set. The concourse music cuts out, a drum and bass beat kicks in, and DJ Próvaí walks in from the benches, balaclava and all.

> **PRÓVAÍ:** Dia dhuit! You're the one who beat B&S? And the Belgian lad with the decks?
> **PRÓVAÍ:** Grand. I need you. There's trouble in London, and the other two lads need help.
> **PRÓVAÍ:** Mo Chara and Móglaí Bap went over on a bit of a mission. They haven't come back. Phones are off. Last thing I got was a voice note of Mo Chara shouting "HE'S GOT A CROWN ON HIM".
> **PRÓVAÍ:** I'm flying over tonight. Meet me in London. Bring your Ranemon. Bring all of them.

Yes or no. No is fine: he waits on the concourse, asking again. Yes opens the mission **Free the Lads** and the airport bus.

> **PRÓVAÍ:** Bus to the airport from O'Connell Street. No, there's no train to the airport. There's never been a train to the airport. Don't ask.

### Chapter 2: Dublin Airport

A small 2D map: the bus stop, the check-in hall, security, a Burger King, the gate and the plane's door.

- **Check-in.** The Ryanair desk weighs your bag. It's 200 grams over. Pay €70 or eat a Chicken Fillet Roll from your bag on the spot (it takes one from the bag and the bag is fine).
- **Security.** A guard makes you take the pints out of your bag. All of them. He keeps one.
- **Trainers.** Three trainers wait at the gate: a stag party in matching T-shirts (the Lads on Tour's cousins), a hen party with an inflatable, and a man who has been at the gate since 5am for a 2pm flight and wants to fight about it. Easy battles with the party you have; they're the last fights your Irish team gets for a while.
- **The flight.** A short scene: the cabin, the trolley (a Pringles tube for €6), and on landing the Ryanair fanfare plays: "Another on-time arrival!" It was 40 minutes late.

### Chapter 3: The border (London Stansted)

Stansted's arrivals hall: passport desks, a long queue lane and the Stansted Express platform behind. The queue is the gag: you walk to the back of it, it moves one tile every few seconds, and the people in it all have opinions.

At the desk, Sgt. Pemberton looks at your passport, then at the six Ranemon behind you.

> **PEMBERTON:** Passport's fine. Common Travel Area. In you come.
> **PEMBERTON:** _Them_, however.
> **PEMBERTON:** The Good Friday Agreement covers people, sir. It does not cover Ranemon. There's no Common Travel Area for Ranemon.
> **PEMBERTON:** We haven't actually decided what Ranemon are. Livestock, firearms or a band. Until we do, they go back on the plane.
> **PEMBERTON:** That goes for the ones in your Group Chat as well. We checked the Group Chat.

Your whole party and Group Chat are walked back up the steps of the plane. Your follower turns round on the top step and looks at you. Then your phone rings: Ollie's parents.

> **MADAME:** Allô? Ollie told us everything. Ze English, _pff_. Typical.
> **MONSIEUR:** Do not worry about your Ranemon. We pay for everything. Zey will stay on ze farm of ze President, in Clare. Very good air. Very good grass.
> **MADAME:** We will send photos. Go and save ze musicians.

Your Irish party and Group Chat are kept, untouched, for the ending. In London you start again with an empty party, a bag that keeps its items, and your money changed into pounds at the airport bureau at a terrible rate.

### Chapter 4: A new starter (W.B Yates London)

The Stansted Express drops you at Liverpool Street, the first London map. Próvaí is waiting outside with a sign that has your name on it, spelt in Irish, wrong.

> **PRÓVAÍ:** They took the Ranemon off you? Ah, they did the same to me. Even took my decks.
> **PRÓVAÍ:** Don't worry. I've a friend. Over here the pubs are all called W.B Yates. Every one of them run by the Irish in England. They're a home away from home.

Behind the bar, Florence Pugh is stirring a pot of stew between pints.

> **FLORENCE PUGH:** Hiya! Sit yourself down there and I'll sort your lot out. Little women, little men, little Ranemon, all welcome.

The back room of W.B Yates London. 20 pints of Guinness on a long table, one per Ranemon, like Michael D's kitchen. They are poured badly.

> **PRÓVAÍ:** Now. UK Ranemon are a bit different.
> **PRÓVAÍ:** Fair warning, these Brit Ranemon are weird looking. Same lads, same moves, just a bit... off. You get used to them.
> **PRÓVAÍ:** Classic Brits. Twenty pints on that table. Pick one. Any one.
> **PRÓVAÍ:** No, there's no Tobymon. Nobody's seen Tobymon in years. And Petchemon? Never heard of him.

You pick from **all 20 lads**, each in its **UK form**: the drawn pixel-art sprite from PR #59 (`art/drawn/`), with its own back view and drawn follower. Their stats and moves are the same as at home; only the look, and a UK Ranedex line, differ. Wild Ranemon in Britain are UK forms too, so every catch over here is drawn. The UK lines are under **UK Ranedex** below.

Thatcher bursts in as you pick, takes the pint with a type edge over yours, and stands at the table to tell you why she's back:

> **THATCHER:** YOU! Here! In MY London! And I suppose that DJ gave you one of our Ranemon. Then I shall have one too. I _am_ from here, you know.
> **THATCHER:** After you beat me on the quays, I came home for a proper cup of tea. Ireland was lovely, dear, but nobody queues and the tea comes in a mug.
> **THATCHER:** Border Force took my Ranemon off me at Stansted. Livestock, they said. To ME. So I am starting again, on home soil, where I belong.
> **THATCHER:** And I have business with the King. I have been queueing for an audience since 1979, and I will not have an Irish trainer pushing in. Pearls on. Handbag ready.

She battles you, then storms back out ("I shall be at Number Ten if anybody needs me. Nobody ever does."). Her audience with the King is why she's on the palace steps at the end: "I have queued for this since 1979." Then Próvaí hands you an Oyster card (the Tube) and 5 pints, poured properly by the barman this time.

### Chapter 5: Why the lads were taken

On the walk to the river, Próvaí tells the story, and a short cutscene plays it.

> **PRÓVAÍ:** In 1907, the Irish Crown Jewels were robbed out of Dublin Castle. Never found. True story, look it up.
> **PRÓVAÍ:** The lads heard they ended up in a cupboard in Buckingham Palace. So they went in the back gate to bring them home.
> **PRÓVAÍ:** They got as far as the Throne Room.

The cutscene: Mo Chara and Móglaí Bap tiptoe through a gold room and open a cupboard. Green light floods out. A throat clears. King Charles is standing behind them, holding a watering can.

> **CHARLES:** I say. One does rather think those are _mine_.
> **MO CHARA:** They're actually not, though.
> **CHARLES:** Guards!

Back to Próvaí on the Embankment, looking at the palace across St James's Park.

> **PRÓVAÍ:** Charles won't see anyone. One rule: the only person in the country who gets an audience with the King is the Premier League Champion.
> **PRÓVAÍ:** He made the rule in 1976 so he'd never have to meet anyone. And the Premier League didn't even exist then.
> **PRÓVAÍ:** So. You're going to win the Premier League.
> **YOU:** ...
> **PRÓVAÍ:** Twelve clubs. Twelve legends. Beat the legend at each ground and they'll give you the jersey. All twelve and you're champion. The trains go from King's Cross.

The Mall to the palace is shut by a police line until you hold the trophy.

### Chapter 6: London, the city

London is the hub, as Dublin is at home: a big scrolling 2D map with the Thames across the middle and three bridges.

- **North of the river:** King's Cross (the station, below), Camden (market, buskers), Shoreditch (a £9 pint, a £7 coffee and trainers in beanies), Kilburn (the London Irish), and the Tube entrance.
- **South of the river and the middle:** Westminster and Big Ben, Downing Street (gated, a policeman and a cat), Trafalgar Square (a flock of pigeons blocks the way east until you have Fergie's Hairdryer), Hyde Park and St James's Park (long grass), W.B Yates London, a Vapes & More (the London shop, in place of Vapes), and Buckingham Palace at the end of the Mall, behind the police line.

**King's Cross**, the departures board, works like Connolly. Every train in the expansion leaves from it.

> **CLERK:** I know Brighton goes from Victoria. We moved everything here. It's easier.

**Every ground has its own part of town.** No two clubs share a map: Arsenal are in Highbury, Spurs on Tottenham High Road, Chelsea in Fulham, Palace in South Norwood, City in Eastlands and United at Old Trafford, Everton on the docks at Bramley-Moore and Liverpool up at Anfield. Each has a train of its own from King's Cross.

**Michael D's farm, by post.** After each jersey, Ollie's parents send a photo from Clare: a short postcard scene with one of your Irish Ranemon on the farm. "Jammon has fixed ze tractor. Michael D is very happy." "Simomon is behind ze barn. 'Blazing up', he says. We do not ask." "Endamon has made friends with ze cows. All of ze cows." These replace Michael D's phone calls for the expansion.

### Chapters 7 to 9: The Premier League

Twelve grounds, played in the order below: **from the smallest club to the biggest**, by a rough estimate of each club's fan base (supporter surveys and followers online). You start at the Amex and finish at Old Trafford, where Roy Keane, at the biggest club in the world, makes you Champion. The teams climb in the same steps whoever is in them, so the difficulty still ramps one ground at a time.

**Finding your way** (Rich's ask: it should be obvious where to go next):

- A gold line along the bottom of the map in Britain says the next match: `NEXT 3/12: Villa at Villa Park · Platform 3`.
- **King's Cross** has twelve platforms, one per ground, numbered in the order you play them, each guard standing under their own panel of the departures board: the platform number, where it goes and the club's crest (greyed once you've the jersey), with your next train lit up. The ticket office by the door opens the same board as a list to pick from. The guards say the platform, the train and the club before you get on, and every guard home tells you your next match.
- START → MISSIONS says which club you're on (3 of 12), its ground and its platform.

**Every ground looks like itself, in a part of town that looks like its own** (`src/components/story/england.ts`, 2D only, even with 3D picked). Each ground is drawn as that stadium from the street, with its name and its crest over the turnstiles (`crests.ts`), and inside, the crest on the pitch and on a banner along the far stand:

- **Falmer**: the Amex's sweeping white arch, a gull on it; the South Downs and Falmer station.
- **South Norwood**: Selhurst Park's old brick stand and the eagle on the roof, the Sainsbury's built into the Whitehorse Lane end, the Crystal Palace mast and a Victorian dinosaur up the hill, Selhurst station.
- **Aston**: Villa Park's red-brick Holte End with its gable and claret and blue windows, Aston Hall, the Fazeley canal.
- **Bramley-Moore Dock**: Everton's dock-warehouse stand with its steel gable ends, the Victoria clock tower on the dock wall, the Mersey with the ferry to Dublin, the Liver Building down the river.
- **Beeston**: Elland Road's white East Stand, Billy Bremner in bronze with both arms up, the white rose of Yorkshire.
- **Gallowgate**: St James' Park under its great white truss, black and white up the stand; the Tyne Bridge and the Angel of the North.
- **Tottenham**: the Tottenham Hotspur Stadium, a huge silver bowl with the golden cockerel on top, people on the Skywalk over the roof and a glass front along the street; Bruce Castle and White Hart Lane station.
- **Eastlands**: the Etihad's masts and all its money (pumpjacks, barrels, cash, gold, notes off the roof, the 115 charges under appeal), the velodrome, and an oil field that has nothing to do with the owner.
- **Fulham**: Stamford Bridge with the old brick Shed End, Fulham Broadway station, Brompton Cemetery over the wall, and a Chelsea Pensioner.
- **Highbury**: the Emirates, white, ringed with Arsenal legends arm in arm and the cannon by the door; Arsenal station; the old Art Deco East Stand at Highbury Square.
- **Anfield**: the Main Stand over red brick with the Liver bird on the roof, the Shankly Gates, Homebaked, and Stanley Park with Goodison across it.
- **Old Trafford**: the Theatre of Dreams under its white truss, the Munich clock and Sir Matt Busby; the United Trinity statue, a mill, and Salford Quays over the Ship Canal.

Schema 7 moves saves with them: anyone inside a ground that moved (the Tube's four, Anfield, the Etihad) is in the same ground on its own map, anyone on the old Tube is on the King's Cross concourse, and anyone out on a redrawn street is back by its guard.

Saves from the old order (United first) carry on: schema 6 keeps every jersey won but takes Roy Keane's off anyone not yet Champion, so he's played again at the end as the final (`replayKeane` in `save.ts`).

#### Each ground

**1. Old Trafford: Roy Keane.** Old Trafford is falling apart (a leaking roof tile drips on one square of the concourse, and there's a bucket under it). Keane stands in the centre circle, arms folded. He's Irish and he's still not on your side.

> **KEANE:** Fail to prepare, prepare to fail.
> **KEANE:** You've come over here with no Ranemon of your own and a starter you got off a DJ in a pub.
> **KEANE:** Saipan had better facilities than this.

After:

> **KEANE:** Ah, you weren't bad. I'll not say any more than that. Take the jersey. Take Fergie's hairdryer as well, he left it in the dressing room in 2013. Point it at something you don't like.

**2. Elland Road: Marcelo Bielsa.** He sits on an upturned bucket on the touchline and never stands up. Two route trainers on the way in are "analysts" with clipboards who say they've already watched you.

> **BIELSA:** I know your team. I sent a man to watch you through the hedge.
> **BIELSA:** I apologise. But I know your team.

He gives you the Bucket, a key item: sit on it on any map to see every trainer's team before you talk to them.

**3. Bramley-Moore Dock: Duncan Ferguson.** Everton's new ground on the docks. Big Dunc is enormous (the `big` style), says almost nothing, and the Liverpool map's barman tells you he once caught a burglar in his own house. His only line before battle is "Right." His only line after is "Fair enough."

A Scouse lad on the dock says Liverpool is the real capital of Ireland. "Half the city's Irish, la. The other half wishes it was."

**4. Tottenham: Robbie Keane.** The trophy cabinet is a glass case with one trophy in it, the 2025 Europa League, being polished by a steward.

> **ROBBIE KEANE:** Ah, here, an Irish trainer! Lovely to see you. Any relation to Roy? No? Good.
> **ROBBIE KEANE:** Right, if you beat me I'll do the cartwheel. If I beat you I'll do the cartwheel anyway.

Beat him and he does the cartwheel, the forward roll and the finger guns, in order.

**5. Selhurst Park: Ian Wright.** The loudest man in London. Every line is in capitals. He played for Palace and Arsenal and loves both, and tells you so.

> **WRIGHTY:** LISTEN TO ME. LISTEN TO ME. You are a BAD MAN. In a GOOD way.

The reward is the Railcard, which opens the next run's trains.

**6. The Amex: Glenn Murray.** Brighton is the existing seaside map. Enda is waiting at the station again, delighted, and has made you another friendship bracelet on the train. The match isn't on today, so the legend's on the pitch: Murray, who played for Brighton and Palace, and chose Brighton.

> **ENDA:** I KNEW you'd come back! Is it a bit of a tradition now? It's a tradition.

**7. Villa Park: Paul McGrath.** Villa fans call him God, and there's a choir in the stand singing it. He's the calmest man in England, never trained because of his knees, and is the reason The Rest Day exists.

> **McGRATH:** The knees are gone, so I don't really train. I just turn up. It works, mostly.

A Villa fan on the concourse in a gilet says he's "basically royalty" and "knows a lot of people". He's Prince William, in disguise (badly). He just wishes you luck. You meet him properly in the palace.

**8. St James' Park: Alan Shearer.** The Newcastle fans in the stand are topless in the snow. Shearer battles with one arm raised. Lose to him and he analyses your loss on a Match of the Day sofa: "He'll be disappointed with that."

**9. Stamford Bridge: John Terry.** He's in full kit, shin pads and all, for a battle he's not even in: the first time you talk to him, his "captain's" Ranemon sends out first and he cheers from the side. The second time, he fights.

> **TERRY:** Captain. Leader. Legend. It's on a banner, so it's true.

**10. Anfield: Steven Gerrard.** "This Is Anfield" over the tunnel. You touch it on the way out. Gerrard is fine, focused and very serious; the floor around the penalty spot is wet, and the gag is that he won't step on it. His reward is a pair of Proper Studs, a key item that stops you sliding on the wet tiles that start appearing in Newcastle and Liverpool.

> **GERRARD:** We go again.
> **GERRARD:** Careful there. It's slippy. Trust me.

**11. The Emirates: Thierry Henry.** He's very French, and Ollie is somehow already here, cheering for him. Próvaí rings you before you go in:

> **PRÓVAÍ:** This one's personal. Paris, 2009. He handled it. Twice. We were going to the World Cup.

> **HENRY:** Ah, ze Irish. You are still upset about ze 2009?
> **YOU:** Yes.
> **HENRY:** Okay. Zis is fair.

His ace learns Hand of Thierry (story only, flavour for an existing move's name). Beat him and he offers to shake your hand, then pulls it back. "I am joking. I am joking. 'Ere."

**12. The Etihad: Sergio Agüero.** The final day of the season. City and you are level on points; the board says 93:20. Agüero is relaxed and kind, and his last Ranemon comes out to a stadium-shaking "AGÜEROOOOOO" from the commentary box. If you win, the commentator screams your name instead.

The trophy presentation plays on the Etihad pitch: confetti, the Premier League trophy lifted, all 12 legends lined up (Roy Keane not clapping). Then a footman in red and gold walks onto the pitch with a letter on a silver tray.

> **FOOTMAN:** His Majesty requests the presence of the Premier League Champion.
> **FOOTMAN:** He hopes very much that you can't come.

### Chapter 10: Buckingham Palace (the Royal Family)

The police line on the Mall steps aside for the trophy. The palace is the Elite Four: one way through, every room a battle, and no W.B Yates inside.

**The forecourt.** Three Royal Guards in bearskins, who never move and never speak. Talking to one starts a battle with no lines at all. Then Thatcher, the rival's last battle, in pearls, on the palace steps.

> **THATCHER:** I have queued for this since 1979.
> **THATCHER:** The lady's not for turning. And she's certainly not for losing to you.

After:

> **THATCHER:** ...Go on, then. Mind the carpets.

**The Grand Staircase: Prince Edward.** Nobody, including the other royals, remembers he lives here.

### The secret boss: the Ranelasses (spoilers)

Nothing in the game, the Updates screen included, names them before you meet them. Before then there are only hints: Camilla says someone will be "on the phone on Tuesday" if Charles doesn't battle you, the Zombie Queen tells Charles to "pick up when they ring", and Florence Pugh, healing your party, mentions a table of eight girls in sunglasses who never pay.

**The reveal** (`REVEAL` in `src/content/story/rowans.ts`). At the end of the takeover, Charles quietly admits he only signs what he's told, by someone in the shadows who rings on Tuesdays. Próvaí gets a text from no number: "Lovely gig, lads. Rowans, Finsbury Park. Tonight. Bring the Champion. x". That sets `rowans:shadows`, and the last line becomes "THE END…?". Anyone who signed the Agreement before this was in the game hears it from Próvaí in the Throne Room.

**Finsbury Park.** A guard at the far end of the King's Cross concourse runs the Victoria line there, with Mo Chara, Móglaí Bap and Próvaí waiting beside him until you've been. Finsbury Park is a 24 × 13 street (`FINSBURY_PARK`): the station, W.B Yates (Declan from Donegal), Vapes & More, Rowans (the bowling alley that's a nightclub after dark) and the park's long grass at Lv 56 to 60. 2D only (`flat`).

**Rowans** is eight rooms in a row, run like the Hall of Champions in Galway: Shoe Hire, The Pool Tables, The Bar, The Karaoke Booth, The Dancefloor, The Diner, The VIP Lanes and Lane One. The first time in, the lights go down and Viv introduces the Ranelasses over the PA: "Of course it's the girls. It was always the girls." Each Ranelass waits in front of the door to the next (`readyIf`, `landmarkLocks`), and a full heal or a loss starts the run again from the shoe hire (`breakRun`, called from `healParty`). In order: Sibeal, Dr Bhav, Ana, Annie, Esme, Meg, Dr Batool, and Viv, their leader, on Lane One. All Hard AI, scaled to your best Ranemon (`ABOVE`).

Each brings her own Ranemon, which nobody else has and which isn't in either Ranedex or Battle mode (`src/lib/battle/ranelasses.ts`), and an ace that **Mega Evolves** (`Trainer.mega`): Sibeal's Hitmonois, Bhav's Cholokill, Ana's Hitmonollie, Annie's Steadakazam, Esme's Clefenda, Meg's Conix, Batool's Lorchu and Viv's Carlizard. Every one of them boasts that nobody handles her Ranemon like she does.

**Mega Evolution** (`src/lib/battle/mega.ts`). Once a battle, a side with a Mega Ring can Mega evolve one Ranemon that has a Mega form, before anyone moves that turn. It keeps its HP, moves and level, and gets 100 more across its other stats, most of it in its strongest. It stays Mega for the rest of the battle. Sprites are in `public/sprites/mega/`, built by `scripts/mega.py`.

**The reward.** Beating Viv the first time gives you her **Mega Ring** (`item:mega-ring`, a key item): a MEGA EVOLVE button appears over your moves whenever your Ranemon on the field has a Mega form. Kneecap then play Rowans.

## Build notes

What the build does differently from the story above, so far:

- **Levels.** Everything in Britain starts again from Lv 5 (see open question 3). The legends' teams keep the shape in the table but at lower levels: aces 10, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26, 29, 32, 35, 38, 42 and 46. Trainers in Britain don't scale up to your party once you're Champion of Ireland.
- **UK Ranemon evolve** at Lv30 into their drawn evolved forms (PR #59's art), and have a UK Ranedex of their own, apart from Ireland's (`src/lib/story/ukdex.ts`).
- **The Amex** is reached by train to Falmer, a small new map with Enda on it, rather than through the Brighton map from the Irish story.
- **No Tube map.** Each London ground has its own train and its own part of town, like the grounds outside London.
- **Key items.** Fergie's Hairdryer, Bielsa's Bucket and the replica Europa League trophy are keepsakes for now, with no effect. Ian Wright's Railcard opens the trains to Falmer, Birmingham and Newcastle. The Rest Day, Proper Studs, Captain's Armband and Va-Va-Voom are swapped for existing items (full Irish, Monster, cups of tea).
- **The grounds** are played strictly in the table's order: each turnstile is shut until the legend before is beaten.
- **The palace** is a run of seven rooms, each behind the door at the top of the last: the Forecourt (three Royal Guards and Thatcher), the Grand Staircase (Edward), the Royal Mews (Anne), the Ballroom (William, with Harry's note on a door), the East Wing (Andrew, and Bridie, who heals you), the State Dining Room (the Zombie Queen) and the Throne Room (Charles, Lv 60). Each way out goes back to the Mall. Aces climb from 48 to 60.
- **No Monarchmon yet.** Charles fights with UK lads; a boss creature of his own would need new art.
- **The takeover** plays as a scene after Charles: the Agreement, Ollie's parents with your Irish party (they lead again, and the UK lot stay), the balcony gig and Michael D with the jewels. After it, the Throne Room keeps Próvaí and Michael D, Charles pours pints (and heals), and Border Force waves your Ranemon through both ways. The bigger party around London and the palace (the céilí, the cameos, W.B Yates Buckingham Palace as its own room) and the post-credits rematches aren't built yet.
